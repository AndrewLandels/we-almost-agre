Take a sentence people actually say. Not a white paper. A slogan.

> Electric cars are shit.

Most of the heat is insult and identity. Once you put it on a map, a surprising amount is already shared, and the leftover is more interesting than the swear word.

## Shared versus contested

### Already shared

A car has to work in ordinary life — journeys, weather, budget, and the time people actually have. Transport has environmental costs, whether the fuel is petrol, diesel, or electricity. And whether an EV is “good enough” depends on where you live, what you drive for, and whether you can charge near home.

If you can say yes to those, you are already on the same side of a large circle.

### Still contested

Three leftovers do the real work:

1. **Total cost** — for most drivers, is an EV worse than a petrol or diesel car over the years you own it?
2. **Charging** — is public charging too thin and unreliable for people without a driveway?
3. **Lifecycle** — once you count batteries, mining, and the electricity mix, are EVs not meaningfully cleaner?

Those are testable claims. They are not the same as “you are a fool for liking cars.”

## The Venn

Picture two circles. One is “this has to work for real people.” The other is “every drivetrain has a footprint.” The overlap is large — on the seed map, about two-thirds. The insult sits outside the overlap, which is why the internet sounds like a fight when it is often a spreadsheet argument plus a charging-map argument plus a lifecycle argument, spoken as one growl.

You can open the curated map here: [Electric cars](/claim/electric-cars). Shared premises carry no stakes. Play-money, if you use it, sits only on the leftover.

## If you want to double down

Suppose you have looked at the map and you still care. The next step is not a purchase. It is to name the avenues and industries that sit behind the leftover.

- **Charging and energy retail** — public networks, workplace charging, on-street bays, home chargers, and the reliability people actually meet on a Thursday night.
- **Batteries and materials** — cells, packs, recycling, and the mining story that makes “cleaner” a longer sentence.
- **Vehicles and the used market** — list prices, insurance, tyres, residual values, and whether the second-hand car park is deep enough.
- **The grid and generation** — what the electricity mix does to the lifecycle claim as it cleans up, or does not.

This is a landscape, not a shopping list. Different leftovers point at different corners. A London flat and a Highland cottage are not the same map.

## Paper trade the leftover — then a broker, later

If a contested claim still nags at you, a paper trade is a way to keep score: write down the claim, a time horizon, and what would change your mind. No pounds leave the house.

Later — not in this first run — the product story includes testing ideas without cash, and only after that, if you still want exposure, using an FCA-regulated broker. That is a future path for people who have done the map, not a suggestion to buy a car, a share, or a charging stock today.
