Most public rows are louder than they need to be. Two people share half a sentence and then spend the evening fighting the other half as if it were the whole truth.

**We Almost Agree** is a calmer way through that. You make a statement. We try to split it into what is already shared and what is still contested. You look at the gap. If the leftover still interests you, you can explore the avenues and industries that sit behind it. Later — not in this first run — you might paper-trade the idea, and later still use a regulated broker if you actually want exposure.

That is the whole pitch. Find where you already agree. Test what is left.

## 1. Make a statement

Start with a sentence someone might actually say. “Electric cars are shit.” “You should invest in Bitcoin.” “America doesn’t have freedom of speech.” Spicy is fine. Insults are usually a clue that shared premises and leftover claims have been mashed together.

You can [paste your own on the home page](/), or open a seed map.

## 2. Evaluate the overlap

The useful work is the split:

- **Shared premises** — things a reasonable person can already accept. Nobody stakes these. They stay on the table as common ground.
- **Contested claims** — the leftover. This is where the heat lives, and where a short, fair evidence trail belongs.

A simple Venn-style agreement map makes the overlap visible. Most people are closer than the original slogan suggested.

This first run does that with curated seed maps and a labelled heuristic for free text. Play-money points go only on contested claims — a calibration game, not cash.

## 3. Avenues and industries

If you still want to lean in after you have seen the map, the next question is not “who won?” It is “what world does this leftover sit in?”

A transport claim might open charging networks, batteries, the used-car market, or the grid. A money claim might open custody, payments rails, energy, or regulation. The point is to name the landscape, not to hand you a ticker.

## 4. Paper trade, then a regulated broker — later

Curiosity can become a test. A paper trade is a way to keep score without spending pounds. If, after that, you still want real exposure, the honest path is an FCA-regulated broker — later, on purpose, and never as a tip from a blog post.

This first run does not include paper trading of markets, payments, affiliates, or accounts. The play-money on the site is a local game about contested sentences.

## What we are not doing

We are not dunking. A counter is another claim in the same map, not a personal attack. We are not giving financial, legal, or medical advice. We are not asking you to open an account.

The product is [We Almost Agree](https://wealmostagree.com). The tone is British, warm, and clear. Monetise clarity later — not humiliation.
