**Find where you already agree. Test what's left.**

We Almost Agree is a place to put a public claim on the table without turning it into a loyalty test. Most online fights mix shared premises with a leftover sentence. People then argue identity. We would rather shrink the perceived gap.

## Make a statement

You arrive with a sentence. A seed like “Electric cars are shit.” A calmer cousin, such as “EVs are good.” Something you typed yourself on the [home page](/).

We do not need you to be polite first. We need the claim in one piece so we can take it apart.

## The site evaluates

The job is a split, not a score for the speaker:

- **Shared premises** — things a reasonable person can already accept. Nobody stakes those.
- **Contested claims** — the leftover. That is where short evidence notes sit, and where play-money can go.
- A simple **agreement map** (the Venn) so you can see the overlap as a shape, not a vibe.

The first run is honest about its limits. Seed maps are curated. Free text goes through a labelled heuristic, in the same shape a later model can fill. It is not a verdict on you, and it is not a source-checked brief until we say it is.

## If you want to lean in

Sometimes the map is enough: you were arguing about two different sentences. Sometimes you still want to lean in — for the leftover, not the person.

Then the useful move is to look at the **avenues and industries** that would make the contested claim true or false. Charging networks. Battery supply. Custody and rails. Platform rules versus statute. Themes you can read around. Not a shopping list of tickers.

## What comes later

Play-money points are here now, in this browser, as a calibration game. They are not cash.

Later, if the product earns it: **paper trading** those themes, then a handoff to an **FCA-regulated broker** for people who still want to act. That path is not live. There is no subscription wall, no affiliate ribbon, and no “buy this” button.

We Almost Agree is trying to make disagreement smaller and clearer — not to manufacture a bigger fight, and not to monetise humiliation.
